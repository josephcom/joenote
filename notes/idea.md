# Idea

*Style: ASD-STE100, Issue 9 — Simplified Technical English.*

## 1. What an idea is

An idea is a pattern. The brain finds this pattern in many experiences. We call this pattern a thread. Example: you see fruit that falls, rain that falls, and persons that fall. The brain keeps the common part. This common part is the idea of "fall".

## 2. How the brain makes threads

The brain is a prediction machine. It always tries to know what comes next. When a prediction fails, the brain learns. Threads come from three sources:

- The world. Example: "zebra".
- The body. Example: "hunger".
- The brain, when it watches its own work. Examples: "not", "if", "same".

## 3. Theories that we tested

Theory 1: an idea is a connection between two things. Result: too weak. Many new ideas only put a new object into an old template.

Theory 2: 65 basic words make all ideas. Result: it worked for "borrow" and "promise". It failed for "red". You cannot build "red" from words. You can only point at red things.

Theory 3: ideas are threads. Result: this theory passed our tests. It also explains why the first two failed.

## 4. Words and ideas

Words and ideas make a loop:

1. A person has a thread with no name. Your "hand-off" thread came before you knew the word.
2. The person makes a word for the thread.
3. The word can then install the thread in other persons. Direct experience is not necessary.
4. Persons connect words and make new threads. Some new threads have no source in experience. Example: "seventeen".

## 5. Roads

Words that occur together make roads. A road is a chain of thoughts that many brains share. Example: "doctor" leads to "hospital" and "nurse". Thought on a road is fast and easy.

Thought off the road is slow, and it looks strange. Example: "doctor plus subscription" was a strange pair in 1990. Today it is telemedicine, a new road.

## 6. Why excellent ideas are rare

The possible combinations of threads are almost without limit. Most combinations are useless. Near combinations are on the road. All persons try them, so they have no value.

Far combinations look stupid at first. Almost no person tries them. But all large discoveries come from this area. The costs are real:

- Your own brain prefers the known roads.
- Other persons cannot see a difference between an explorer and a fool.
- Most far combinations fail.

An excellent idea is a good location off the road. It is rare because the search is large and the costs are high.

## 7. How to find new ideas

1. Fill your mind with the problem. This prepares the search.
2. Make combinations of distant threads. Try 10 to 20.
3. Keep only the combinations that apply to your goal.
4. Sleep. The brain continues the search.

## 8. Open question

Is an AI also a machine of threads? Can it make excellent ideas? We did not examine this. That is the next step.
