# Leading Indicators (IEL)

**The rule of this file:** every node is a number you can read on a public page. Tap the link, see the figure. If something has no page and no number, it is not in a chain.

**How to read:** `→` means "this number moves before that number". The last node in each chain is the one closest to IDP's revenue. Under each chain: **What** defines the indicators, **How it predicts** gives the mechanics, and **Lead** says how far ahead the warning comes.

**One thing to hold on to before you start.** IDP's revenue is volume × price in three product lines. It has no capacity limit of its own. Its volume is the students that six foreign governments let through, so a quota is a ceiling on IDP's demand, not a limit on IDP's supply — and today the quotas do not bind, because refusals and student appetite hold the numbers well below them. IDP's supply side is its rivals, plus every English test a government adds to its accepted list. So most of this file reads immigration policy, not company operations.

# Market level

**M1 — Can the family pay?**
[Daily exchange rates](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/frequency/exchange-rates.html) → [Student visa fee and savings threshold](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500) → [Indian outward remittances for study](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx) → [Australian student visa lodgements](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/student-visas)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Daily exchange rates](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/frequency/exchange-rates.html) | Daily | Every business day; the trade-weighted index at 4pm |
| [Student visa fee and savings threshold](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500) | Yearly | Fees change on 1 July with the Budget cycle; the savings test is indexed, usually around October |
| [Indian outward remittances for study](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx) | Monthly | About the 25th, for data two months old. Table 36 of the RBI bulletin |
| [Australian student visa lodgements](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/student-visas) | Monthly | About five weeks after the month it covers |

**What:** The RBA page gives AUD/INR, AUD/GBP and AUD/USD every business day. The visa fee is what the government charges to apply; Australia's rose to AUD 2,500 on 1 July 2026. The savings threshold is the money a student must prove they hold — AUD 29,710 for a year, plus more for a partner and each child, held for three months before applying. Table 36 of the RBI monthly bulletin carries a line called "studies abroad": the actual dollars Indian families send out for education.
**How it predicts:** The family earns in rupees and the university charges in dollars. If the Australian dollar rises, the same degree costs more in rupees, and nobody has changed a price. Add the visa fee and the savings test and there is a hard cash gate before an application is even possible. Education remittances from India fell **20.9% in FY26 to US$2.31bn**. The money leaves before the student does.
**Lead:** the remittance line runs one to two quarters ahead of a commencement. The exchange rate leads the remittance line by about a quarter. Fee and threshold changes are known months ahead.

**M2 — Is the degree still worth it?**
[UK Graduate Route](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa) and [Australian Temporary Graduate visa](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/temporary-graduate-485) and [Canadian post-graduation work permit](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/eligibility.html) → [IDP Emerging Futures](https://careers.idp.com/news/emerging-futures-research-test) → student visa applications

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [UK Graduate Route](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa) | As policy changes | Cut from 24 months to 18 on **1 January 2027**. PhD stays at 36 |
| [Australian Temporary Graduate visa](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/temporary-graduate-485) | As policy changes | Age limit 35, English at IELTS 6.5. Fee rose to AUD 5,750 on 1 July 2026 |
| [Canadian post-graduation work permit](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/eligibility.html) | As policy changes | Field-of-study list frozen 15 Jan 2026; the freeze runs to the end of 2026 |
| [IDP Emerging Futures](https://careers.idp.com/news/emerging-futures-research-test) | About twice a year | Edition 9 landed 13 May 2026. Edition 10 due about October or November 2026 |

**What:** The three work-right pages say how long a graduate may stay and work after the course ends. Emerging Futures is IDP's own survey of student intent — 5,800 students in 118 countries in the last edition.
**How it predicts:** A student buys a foreign degree partly for the job that follows it. Shorten the work visa and you cut the return on the whole investment, so the student either picks another country or does not go. Emerging Futures now finds **78%** of students consider more than one country before committing, up from 66% in October 2024, and among students who abandoned their plans **27%** blamed visa costs and **26%** blamed visa difficulty.
**Lead:** a work-rights change is announced six to eighteen months before it starts, and applications react at the next intake. The UK cut is the live one: 1 January 2027, so the damage shows in the September 2027 intake. It is IDP's own survey, so treat it as directional, not as an independent reading.

**M3 — The feedstock**
Chinese gaokao registrations → Indian students going abroad → [Visa grants by nationality](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/immigration-statistics-quarterly-release)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese gaokao registrations | Yearly | Announced in early June, for that year's exam. **No standing page.** The number reaches the world through Chinese state media |
| Indian students going abroad | Yearly | Released through answers in the Indian parliament, usually December or February. **No standing page** |
| [Visa grants by nationality](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/immigration-statistics-quarterly-release) | Quarterly | Late February, May, August and November |

**What:** The gaokao is China's university entrance exam, and the registration count is the size of the cohort. The Indian number comes from the Bureau of Immigration and reaches the public through answers in parliament.
**Warning:** the first two nodes break this file's own rule, because neither has a page you can bookmark. They are here because the numbers are real and nothing else measures the same thing. Take each reading from the news of the day and write it into journal.md, so the series gets built here instead.
**How it predicts:** No marketing fills a cohort that does not exist. Gaokao registrations fell from 13.35m in 2025 to **12.9m in 2026**. Indian students abroad fell from about 908,000 in 2023 to **626,000 in 2025**, a 31% fall in two years. These are slow and near-certain.
**Lead:** one to four years. This is the longest chain in the file and the least useful for a trading decision, but it is what sets the ceiling.

**M4 — Where else would they go?**
[Navitas Agent Perception Report](https://www.navitas.com/naps/) → [Studyportals enrolment benchmark](https://studyportals.com/news-and-insights/) → destination shares inside the visa data

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Navitas Agent Perception Report](https://www.navitas.com/naps/) | Yearly | About August. Round 10 landed 18 August 2026, from 870 agents in 64 countries |
| [Studyportals enrolment benchmark](https://studyportals.com/news-and-insights/) | Twice a year, by intake | About March for the January intake and late in the year for the September intake |

**What:** Navitas asks education agents how they rate each destination. Studyportals asks universities what their own applications and enrolments are doing. Both are surveys, not counts, so they belong at the front of a chain and never at the end.
**How it predicts:** Agents move students before the students move. When agents rate a destination down, applications there fall the next intake. Round 10 found perceptions stabilising for Australia, improving for Canada, still sliding for the UK, worst for the US, and New Zealand beating all four. Studyportals found English-speaking destinations falling while Europe and Asia grew. That is a mix shift, and IDP sells only the six English-speaking destinations.
**Lead:** one to two intakes, so roughly six to twelve months.

**M5 — The graduate-study proxy**
[GMAT volumes](https://www.gmac.com/market-intelligence-and-research) → English test volumes

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [GMAT volumes](https://www.gmac.com/market-intelligence-and-research) | Yearly | About late October, for the testing year to 30 June |

**What:** GMAC publishes how many GMAT exams were taken, split by the test taker's citizenship. India is about a quarter of them.
**How it predicts:** A student who sits a GMAT is preparing a postgraduate application abroad, and will sit an English test in the same season. Global GMAT volume fell **19% to 93,196** in the year to June 2025. It is a small sample against IDP's 1.2m tests, but it is one of very few independent reads on the same intent.
**Lead:** one to two quarters ahead of the English test sitting; two to four quarters ahead of the enrolment.

# Sector level

**S1 — Australia: the quota and the queue.** Australia is 58% of IDP's placements, so this is the most important chain in the file.
[National Planning Level](https://www.dewr.gov.au/international-skills-engagement/managed-system-international-education-2026) → [Ministerial Direction and processing priority](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-processing-times/visa-processing-priorities/student-visa) → [Visa lodgements, grants and grant rate](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/student-visas) → [Enrolments and commencements](https://www.education.gov.au/international-education-data-and-research/international-student-monthly-summary-and-data-tables)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [National Planning Level](https://www.dewr.gov.au/international-skills-engagement/managed-system-international-education-2026) | Yearly | About July or August, for the following calendar year |
| [Ministerial Direction and processing priority](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-processing-times/visa-processing-priorities/student-visa) | As policy changes | MD 115 replaced MD 111 on 14 November 2025 |
| [Visa lodgements, grants and grant rate](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/student-visas) | Monthly | About five weeks after month end. Spreadsheet download, and the page often refuses robots |
| [Enrolments and commencements](https://www.education.gov.au/international-education-data-and-research/international-student-monthly-summary-and-data-tables) | Monthly | Six to ten weeks behind. The site blocks robots |

**What:** The planning level is the number of new overseas student commencements the government will allow, split into an allocation for each provider. It is **295,000 for 2026 and unchanged for 2027**. Ministerial Direction 115 sorts providers into three processing tiers by how much of their allocation they have used: under 80% is fastest, over 115% is slowest. The visa dataset gives lodgements, grants and the grant rate by sector, by citizenship and by whether the applicant is onshore or offshore. The education data gives enrolments and commencements once the student is actually here.
**How it predicts:** This is a quota, so the ceiling is legislated and known a year ahead. Under the ceiling, the government controls flow with the refusal rate, which it can move without announcing anything. Read **lodgements** first, because they are demand, then the **grant rate**, because that is the government's hand on the tap. In February 2026 the refusal rate hit **32.5%**, the highest in 21 years. By July 2026 higher-education grants were **40%** below July 2025 and the offshore grant rate was 66%. Commencements are a confirmation, not a warning — the visa data already said it two months earlier.
**Lead:** lodgements lead grants by four to twelve weeks depending on the provider's tier, and grants lead commencements by one to four months. For the dominant February intake, watch October to December lodgements. Total warning: about two quarters.

**S2 — The UK: the fastest number anywhere in this file**
[Monthly entry clearance visa applications](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-july-2026/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-july-2026) → [Quarterly immigration statistics, study](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/immigration-statistics-quarterly-release) → [HESA student statistics](https://www.hesa.ac.uk/data-and-analysis/students)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Monthly entry clearance visa applications](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-july-2026/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-july-2026) | Monthly | About 13 days after month end, 09:30. August data is due **10 September 2026** |
| [Quarterly immigration statistics, study](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/immigration-statistics-quarterly-release) | Quarterly | Last Thursday of February, May, August and November, 09:30 |
| [HESA student statistics](https://www.hesa.ac.uk/data-and-analysis/students) | Yearly | Late January, about seven months behind. The site refuses every automated fetch |

**What:** The monthly release counts sponsored study **applications** on a rolling twelve-month basis. The quarterly release counts **grants**, split by nationality, study level and dependants, and it also counts Graduate Route grants. HESA counts students actually enrolled. Note there is **no collection page for the monthly release**: each month gets its own URL of the shape `.../monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-<month>-<year>` and the old page never updates, so change the month in the link. Forward dates sit on the [statistics finder](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/announcements), filtered to the Home Office.
**How it predicts:** An application is a decision the student has already made and paid for. Thirteen days after a month closes you can see whether it happened. In the year to July 2026 sponsored study applications ran **381,500, down 11%**; grants in the year to June 2026 were **383,455, down 11%, and 41% below the 2023 peak**. Two dated UK changes are already in the diary: the Graduate Route drops to 18 months on 1 January 2027, and a **£925 per student levy** on English universities starts in the 2028-29 academic year, which the government itself expects to cut enrolments by 14,000 to 16,500.
**Lead:** about one quarter to enrolment, so roughly two to three quarters to IDP's revenue. Nothing else in this file is this fast or this clean.

**S3 — Canada: the cap and the attestation letters**
[Immigration Levels Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/mandate/corporate-initiatives/levels.html) → [Provincial attestation letter allocations](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/notices.html) → [Study permit holders, monthly open data](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/90115b00-f9b8-49e8-afa3-b4cff8facaee)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Immigration Levels Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/mandate/corporate-initiatives/levels.html) | Yearly | **By 1 November each year**, by law. The 2027 target is confirmed or changed then |
| [Provincial attestation letter allocations](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/notices.html) | Yearly | About late November. The 2026 notice was 25 November 2025 |
| [Study permit holders, monthly open data](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/90115b00-f9b8-49e8-afa3-b4cff8facaee) | Monthly | Mid to late month, about seven weeks behind |

**What:** The levels plan sets how many study permit holders may arrive: **155,000 in 2026, a notional 150,000 for 2027 and 2028**. The attestation letter is a provincial permission slip a student needs before applying; the provinces share out a fixed number of them. The open data file counts permit **holders** at a point in time.
**How it predicts:** Canada is the clearest case of supply, not demand, setting the number. About **73,800** new permits were issued in 2025 against a target of 305,900 — 24% of it — and the approval rate for Indian applicants fell from 69% to about 26%. IDP's Canadian placements fell **80%** in FY26 and Canada is now a small part of the business. The important date is **1 November 2026**, when the 2027 number becomes real.
**Lead:** permits lead arrival by two to five months. Canada's main intake is September, with January and May behind it.

**S4 — The United States: a broken gauge**
[Monthly nonimmigrant visa issuance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-statistics/nonimmigrant-visa-statistics/monthly-nonimmigrant-visa-issuances.html) → [SEVIS active student records](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-data-mapping-tool) → [IIE Open Doors](https://opendoorsdata.org/annual-release/international-students/) and [the NAFSA outlook](https://www.nafsa.org/fall2026outlook)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Monthly nonimmigrant visa issuance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-statistics/nonimmigrant-visa-statistics/monthly-nonimmigrant-visa-issuances.html) | Nominally monthly | Publication has become erratic. The latest month available is February 2026 |
| [SEVIS active student records](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-data-mapping-tool) | Monthly | Four to six weeks behind. The site refuses automated fetches |
| [IIE Open Doors and snapshots](https://opendoorsdata.org/annual-release/international-students/) | Yearly, plus a spring snapshot | Mid-November, and about July |
| [NAFSA outlook](https://www.nafsa.org/fall2026outlook) | Yearly | August |

**What:** The State Department series counts visas issued by class, so F-1 is readable directly. SEVIS counts student records that are currently active. Open Doors and NAFSA are surveys of universities.
**How it predicts:** Fewer visas, fewer students, and the American chain is short because interviews happen late — one to four months before term. But **the primary series is not working**. State Department data runs about six months behind, and SEVIS is a stock that includes graduates on work authorisation, so it hides the collapse in new arrivals. F-1 issuance fell **34% in 2025**, India by 76% against 2023, and NAFSA projects up to **111,000 fewer students** in autumn 2026. IDP's American placements fell 67% in FY26.
**Lead:** in theory one quarter. In practice the surveys are doing the work the statistics should do, so treat the US as an already-known loss rather than a live signal.

**S5 — New Zealand and Ireland: the release valve, and the blind spot**
[New Zealand migration data explorer](https://mbienz.shinyapps.io/migration_data_explorer/) → arrivals; Ireland has no scheduled release at all

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [New Zealand migration data explorer](https://mbienz.shinyapps.io/migration_data_explorer/) | Monthly | The **second Monday** of each month. It is an interactive app, so no script can read it |
| Irish study visa decisions | None | Numbers surface only through parliamentary answers and the annual immigration review |

**What:** New Zealand publishes student visa applications and decisions by nationality. Ireland publishes nothing on a schedule.
**How it predicts:** New Zealand is the only destination trying to grow — a target of 119,000 enrolments by 2034, work rights lifted to 25 hours a week from 3 November 2025, and a new short graduate work visa due late in 2026. June 2026 was its strongest month for student visa approvals since at least 2016, up 31%. It is small, so read it as a mix signal: it tells you students are still coming, just going elsewhere. Ireland is the opposite problem — the study visa refusal rate went from 22% in 2023 to **64% in the first five months of 2026**, and you would only know by reading a parliamentary answer.
**Lead:** one to three months for New Zealand. None for Ireland.

**S6 — Who pays the commission**
[Financial sustainability of English universities](https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/publications/financial-sustainability-of-higher-education-providers-in-england-2026/) → [Victorian university audit results](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/) → agent commission structures → IDP's placement yield

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Financial sustainability of English universities](https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/publications/financial-sustainability-of-higher-education-providers-in-england-2026/) | Yearly, plus an update | Main report about mid-May; an update about November |
| [Victorian university audit results](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/) | Yearly | Early June, for the previous calendar year |
| [Universities Australia sector reporting](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/stats-publications/university-funding-stats-2/) | Yearly | About late February |
| Agent commission surveys | Yearly | The American agency survey lands about July, reported through [ICEF Monitor](https://monitor.icef.com/) |

**What:** These are the accounts of the customers who pay IDP. The Office for Students report gives the share of English providers in deficit and how far their international recruitment missed their own forecast. The Victorian auditor gives audited results for each university, including international fee revenue.
**How it predicts:** It cuts both ways, and which way matters more than any other question in this file. A university short of students may pay agents *more* to fill seats — which is the bull case for IDP's yield. Or it may cut the commission rate to save cash. The evidence so far favours paying more: **35.8%** of English providers were in deficit in 2024-25 and 42.7% are forecast for 2025-26, international recruitment came in 9.0% under their own forecast, and IDP's commission per placement still rose 11%. Against that, the American survey shows institutions moving from a percentage of tuition to **fixed referral fees of US$2,000 to 3,500**, and a fixed fee stops rising with tuition. If that model reaches the UK and Australia, IDP's yield growth stops structurally.
**Lead:** university accounts are a year old when published, so they confirm rather than warn. The commission-structure surveys are the forward-looking half.

**S7 — The approved-test lists, which can halve the revenue**
[Australia's accepted English tests](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/english-language/competent-english) and [Canada's designated testing agencies](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/standard-requirements/language-requirements/designated-testing-agencies.html) and [the UK's secure English test list](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) → IELTS volumes

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Australia's accepted English tests](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/english-language/competent-english) | As policy changes | Last changed 7 August 2025. Old results stay valid to 6 August 2028 |
| [Canada's designated testing agencies](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/standard-requirements/language-requirements/designated-testing-agencies.html) | As policy changes | Last changed 8 August 2025 |
| [The UK's secure English test list](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) | As policy changes | Page last updated 28 August 2026 |

**What:** Each government publishes the tests it will accept for a visa. Australia accepts nine; the list went from five to nine on 7 August 2025, adding CELPIP, LanguageCert and the Michigan English Test. Australia accepts **no** fully online test, so IELTS Online does not count there. Canada abolished its student list entirely when the Student Direct Stream closed on 8 November 2024, and keeps a list only for work permits. The UK list has four providers inside the UK and four outside.
**How it predicts:** A test that is not on the list earns nothing from visa applicants, no matter how good it is. That is why Duolingo, at a fifth of the price, is not the threat it looks like — it is on none of the three lists. Equally, every test added is share taken from IELTS at the same total volume. Watch the lists, not the marketing.
**Lead:** immediate. A list change moves bookings the same month, and the old-results grace period tells you when the full effect lands.

**S8 — The UK Home Office test contract**
[Find a Tender notices](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/001247-2026) → [the secure English test list](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) → British Council and IDP IELTS volumes

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Find a Tender notices](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/001247-2026) | As the procurement moves | Notice published 28 November 2025. Award decision due **16 November 2026** |
| The new contract | One contract | Written to start **1 December 2026**, five years, with options to 2034 |
| The IELTS bid | Settled | The consortium **withdrew on 17 March 2026** |

**What:** The Home Office is running a procurement worth **£680m** before tax to replace today's four-provider concession with a single supplier and a Home Office-branded remote test. The current concession is the one under which IELTS is a UK visa test.
**How it predicts:** The British Council delivers close to **90%** of the world's UK-visa English tests through the IELTS consortium. The consortium withdrew its bid on 17 March 2026, because it will not deliver a high-stakes test fully remotely. So the winner will not be IELTS, and a very large block of IELTS volume goes when the new supplier takes over. It hits IDP's co-owner directly and IDP's brand indirectly. This is the largest single risk to the testing half of the company. It is now a question of date and size, not of whether.
**Lead:** the award comes first, then the changeover. Watch the notice page for the winner and for the handover date, and watch the approved-test list for the day IELTS leaves it.

**S9 — What the competitors' volumes say**
[Pearson results](https://plc.pearson.com/en-GB/investors/performance) and [Duolingo quarterly filings](https://investors.duolingo.com/financials) and [the British Council annual report](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/britishcouncil_annualreport_2024-25.pdf) → IDP's share

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Pearson results](https://plc.pearson.com/en-GB/investors/performance) | Four times a year | Full year late February, first quarter about 1 May, half year end of July, nine months about 22 October |

**What:** Pearson gives a percentage change in PTE test volume and nothing else. Duolingo gives its test revenue as a line in the accounts and no volume. The British Council gives its own IELTS test count.
**How it predicts:** Subtract. If PTE falls 3% while IDP's IELTS falls 8%, the difference is share, not market. That is exactly what has happened: PTE volumes fell 10%, then 5%, then 3% across 2024, 2025 and the first half of 2026, while IDP's IELTS fell 18% then 8%. Duolingo's test revenue was $42.0m in 2025 and is flat to falling, so it is not where the share is going.
**Lead:** none. This is a diagnostic that tells you whether to blame the market or the company.

**S10 — The rival channel**
[Crizac quarterly results](https://www.tickertape.in/stocks/crizac-CRIZ) → UK market share → IDP's UK placements

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Crizac quarterly results](https://www.tickertape.in/stocks/crizac-CRIZ) | Quarterly | On the Indian calendar. Year ends 31 March |

**What:** Crizac is an India-listed student recruitment platform that listed in July 2025. The UK is 97% of its revenue. It is the only listed pure comparable to IDP's placement business anywhere.
**How it predicts:** In the year to March 2026 Crizac's revenue rose **22.7%** and its share of UK study visas went from 3.5% to **6.0%**, in the same year IDP's revenue fell 11%. Then in the June 2026 quarter Crizac's income fell 4%, its first fall. Read the sequence, not either number alone: part of IDP's FY26 decline was share loss to a cheaper channel, and by mid-2026 the whole market was going down together. Most of IDP's other rivals are private, so this is the only public read on the channel.
**Lead:** Crizac reports quarterly against IDP's half-yearly, so it gives an intermediate read on the UK between IDP's own results.

# Company level

**C1 — The master chain**
[Placement volumes and yield](https://investors.idp.com/Investor-Centre/?page=ASX-Announcements) → [IELTS volumes and yield](https://investors.idp.com/Investor-Centre/?page=ASX-Announcements) → adjusted EBIT

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Placement volumes and yield](https://investors.idp.com/Investor-Centre/?page=ASX-Announcements) | Half-yearly, plus the AGM | Late February and late August. The **AGM is about late October** |
| [IELTS volumes and yield](https://investors.idp.com/Investor-Centre/?page=ASX-Announcements) | Half-yearly, plus the AGM | Same |

**What:** IDP publishes four numbers that explain almost everything: placement volume, commission per placement, test volume and fee per test. It publishes them twice a year and gives directional commentary at the AGM. There is no quarterly report. The announcements index needs a browser; the result PDFs fetch directly.
**How it predicts:** Volume is what the governments allowed. Yield is what IDP charged. In FY26 volumes fell 27% and 8% while yields rose 11% and 7%, which is why adjusted EBIT held at $122.9m on revenue down 11%. Apply the FY27 guidance of **$95-115m** against the assumption of market volumes 20-30% lower, and you have management's own arithmetic to test.
**Lead:** none. This is the answer, not the warning. Everything above exists so you know it before it is printed.

**C2 — Is the yield price, or is it mix?**
Destination mix inside the placement volumes → commission per placement; [the IELTS fee in India](https://ieltsidpindia.com/information/ielts-test-fee) → fee per test

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| Destination mix | Half-yearly | With each result |
| [The IELTS fee in India](https://ieltsidpindia.com/information/ielts-test-fee) | Yearly | Rises have landed on 1 March 2025 and 1 April 2026 |

**What:** The mix is the share of placements going to each destination. The India fee page is a live price list — currently ₹19,000 for a standard test.
**How it predicts:** Australia went from 43% to **58%** of IDP's placements in FY26, and Australia is a higher-value corridor than the ones that collapsed. So a large part of the 11% yield rise is mix, not price. If Australia now falls too, the mix reverses and the yield falls with it even though IDP has raised no fee. The India price page is the honest read on actual pricing power: one rise a year, ₹17,000 to ₹18,000 to ₹19,000.
**Lead:** the fee page moves weeks before a result; the mix is only visible in the result itself.

**C3 — The cost line, which is the only lever management pulls**
Headcount and venue counts → adjusted overheads → break-even revenue

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| Headcount, venue count, adjusted overheads | Half-yearly | With each result and in the annual report |

**What:** Permanent headcount, the number of IELTS venues and the adjusted overhead line. Headcount went from about 6,000 to **4,828** in FY26, a cut of 1,250 roles. Venues went from about 1,500 to fewer than 600.
**How it predicts:** Every dollar out of overheads lowers the revenue at which IDP breaks even. FY26 took $32m out against a $25m target and moved break-even from about $683m to about $590m. Another $15m is planned for FY27, against about **$40m of one-off transformation spend**. Watch whether the "one-off" line ever actually stops — it has now run two years, and it is the whole gap between a $57.1m adjusted profit and a $13.3m statutory one.
**Lead:** cost programs are announced with the result and land over the following year.

**C4 — The partnership, which IDP does not control**
[The British Council annual report](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/britishcouncil_annualreport_2024-25.pdf) and [the audit office report on its loan](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council/) and [the parliamentary inquiry](https://committees.parliament.uk/work/9762/financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council/) → the ownership of IELTS

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [The British Council annual report](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/britishcouncil_annualreport_2024-25.pdf) | Yearly | About late March, for the year to 31 March. The file name carries the year, so change it to get the next one |
| [The audit office report](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council/) | One-off, with follow-ups | Published 17 June 2026 |
| [The parliamentary inquiry](https://committees.parliament.uk/work/9762/financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council/) | As hearings are held | Oral evidence 2 July 2026 |
| [The Cambridge annual report](https://www.cambridge.org/about-us/annual-report) | Yearly | About March or April, for the year to 31 July |

**What:** IELTS is owned jointly by three parties and IDP is one of them. The British Council's accounts and the audit office report show whether that co-owner can continue.
**How it predicts:** The British Council lost £51m in 2024-25, owes the government **£197m**, has repaid no principal since April 2024, and does not expect to return to profit before 2029-30. It has already sold IDP its India IELTS business once, for £130m in 2021, to raise cash. A distressed co-owner is both a risk and an opportunity: it could sell more territory to IDP, or it could sell to someone else. Its chief executive told Parliament in July 2026 that the exams business "is basically based on migration around the world" and slows enormously when migration slows. **Nobody publishes the partnership terms**, so this chain reads the partner's health, not the contract.
**Lead:** the accounts are yearly and the inquiry is irregular. Treat it as a standing risk with no clock, until a transaction is announced.

**C5 — Capital and the register**
Buyback and dividend → [short interest](https://www.shortinterest.au/ticker/iel) → [substantial holder notices](https://investors.idp.com/Investor-Centre/?page=ASX-Announcements)

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| [Short interest](https://www.shortinterest.au/ticker/iel) | Daily, from regulator data | Reported with a four-day delay |
| [Substantial holder notices](https://investors.idp.com/Investor-Centre/?page=ASX-Announcements) | As they happen | Any time |
| The buyback | Running | Up to $50m, from 21 August 2026 to **30 June 2027** |

**What:** Short interest is the share of the company sold short. Substantial holder notices show when a large investor crosses 5%.
**How it predicts:** It does not predict revenue at all, and belongs here only so you never confuse it with demand. Short interest was **2.90% at 31 August 2026**, well down from a 17% peak in May 2024, which means the easy bear trade is largely over. The buyback and the 6.0c final dividend say the board still thinks the balance sheet is comfortable. A takeover approach would end the forecasting exercise overnight.
**Lead:** none.

**C6 — Separating IDP from the market**
IDP's visa approval rate minus the market's → is the fall share loss or market contraction

| Factor | Frequency | When |
|---|---|---|
| IDP's own approval rates | Half-yearly | With each result |
| [The market's grant rates](https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/student-visas) | Monthly | About five weeks after month end |

**What:** IDP publishes its own students' visa approval rate against the market's. In FY26 it claimed 86.3% against an industry 81.2% in Australia, 80.8% against 66.0% for Indian applicants overall, and 59.7% against 29.8% for Indian students going to Canada.
**How it predicts:** This is a diagnostic, not a warning. If IDP's placements fall faster than the destination's visa grants, the loss is IDP's own and you should weight S10 and S9. If they fall together, it is the market, and you should weight S1 to S5. Do the subtraction each half.
**Lead:** none by itself. It tells you which of the chains above to trust.

**Standalone (company)**
- **Sell-side targets** collapsed onto the market price after the FY26 result. UBS moved from Buy to Neutral and cut its target from $5.15 to $1.95; Morgans cut from $3.42 to $1.75. Both now sit at or below the traded price, so the wide spread of early 2026 is gone, and a single upgrade would stand out against that. Targets move the price, not the revenue.
- **The AGM**, about late October, is the only company update between the August and February results, and it usually carries the first read on the February Australian intake.

# Traps

- **FY25 was restated.** A voluntary revenue-recognition change on 18 December 2025 lifted FY25 revenue by $9.2m and moved placement volumes to 73,900 from a reported 70,100. Management quotes FY26 changes against the restated base. Mixing the two bases produces wrong percentages every time.
- **Adjusted is not clean.** FY26 adjusted NPAT was $57.1m and statutory NPAT was $13.3m. The $61.1m gap is mostly restructuring, and about $40m more is guided for FY27. A cost that recurs for three years is not a significant item.
- **A rising yield can be a falling business.** Australia went from 43% to 58% of placements because the other destinations collapsed. Part of the 11% yield rise is that mix. Never quote the yield rise as proof of pricing power without checking the mix.
- **Canada's monthly open data is a stock, not a flow.** It counts permit holders, and extensions were 77% of approvals. Only new permits reach IDP's revenue.
- **The American visa series is stale.** State Department monthly issuance runs about six months behind, and SEVIS counts active records including graduates on work authorisation. Do not read either as a current reading of new arrivals.
- **Announced is not in force.** A migration package restricting student dependants and onshore applications was drafted for a speech on 6 August 2026 and pulled when cabinet did not agree. Until an instrument is made, it is press reporting.
- **More approved tests is share loss at flat volume.** Australia went from five accepted tests to nine on 7 August 2025. IELTS volume can fall with no fall in students.
- **IDP has never published its IELTS stake, the revenue split or any royalty.** The "one third each" figure is trade press. Do not put it in a briefing as a company figure.
- **marketindex.com.au renders IEL as "HALTED SUSPENDED" with a stale June 2025 price.** It is a page artifact, confirmed again on 4 September 2026, and the same fault the GEM file records. The stock trades normally.
- **stockanalysis.com's market cap does not match its own share count.** On 31 August 2026 it showed $505.77m against 277.90m shares at $1.765, which multiplies to $490m. Always compute shares × price yourself.
- **stockanalysis.com's IEL statements stop at FY25 and carry no FY26 at all.** Its "TTM" column is December 2025 and its "Current" column is dated 11 August 2026, which is before the result. Anything it labels FY25 is also the pre-restatement base. Read FY26 out of [the FY26 financial report](https://investors.idp.com/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/v1AiEHYL20-_Rje11PzkYA/financial-reports/FY26-Full-Year-Results.pdf) instead; the combined Appendix 4E and Annual Report PDF truncates before the statements, and that separate file does not.
- **stockanalysis.com counts only plant as capex, and IDP's capex is mostly capitalised intangibles.** It shows FY25 capex of $9.3m and free cash flow of $123.4m against the true $60.9m and $71.7m, so it overstates free cash flow by about 70%. Capex is payments for plant **plus** payments for intangibles and development costs.
- **Australian grant rates saw-tooth from month to month.** The offshore rate ran about 67% in February 2026, 59% in March, 83% in April and 66% in July. Processing priority moves it, not demand. Read lodgements for demand, and read the grant rate over a quarter or more.
- **The Australian and English data pages block robots.** data.gov.au and education.gov.au both refuse automated fetches and serve spreadsheets. HESA refuses everything. Plan to download by hand or read a secondary that quotes them.
