# Operating Leverage
## Calculation
**Example**  
Revenue: $1,000  
Variable cost: $600  
Left over: $400 ← this is contribution margin  
Fixed cost: $300  
EBIT (profit before interest and tax): $100  
True leverage = 400 ÷ 100 = **4×**

## Why does it change from year to year?
Operating leverage changes from year to year because:

1. fixed vs variable costs split changes each year
2. one-off items (like redundancy payouts, legal settlements or asset write-downs etc.) may change it
3. margin per sale can change (higher sell price, lower material, sell different products etc.)

## Real example  
The following shows ASX:GEM figures:  

| Year | Revenue | Fixed cost | Op EBIT | True leverage | Break-even revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY22 | $905.5m | $209.5m | $80.3m | **3.6×** | $654.6m |
| FY23 | $987.0m | $215.2m | $100.6m | **3.1×** | $672.6m |
| FY24 | $1,021.8m | $212.0m | $115.0m | **2.8×** | $662.4m |
| FY25 | $948.2m | $210.1m | $93.3m | **3.3×** | $656.6m |
