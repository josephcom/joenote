# Financial Snapshot (IEL)

**What this file is for.** The chains in `leading-indicators.md` forecast revenue.
They never say what the business is worth, or what it can survive. This file does
that. It is built once, rebuilt yearly, and refreshed at each half-year for net debt,
leverage and cover, because those three move and those three are the ones that kill
you.

**How a briefing uses it.** A briefing does not read the tables. It reads
**the threshold lines at the end** — the level at which the answer changes.

**Currency:** A$ million. **Fiscal year ends 30 June.**

**The definition of invested capital, fixed here and never to be switched:**

> **NOPAT** = statutory EBIT x (1 - 30%).
> **Invested capital** = shareholders' equity + net debt excluding lease liabilities.
> **Goodwill is included.**

Statutory EBIT, not adjusted EBIT, is the base. IDP's adjusting items have run at
$61.1m a year for two years with about $40m more guided for FY27, and a cost that
recurs for three years is not a significant item. The adjusted ROIC is given beside
the statutory one in every year, because the gap between them **is** the question
about this company. Goodwill is included because IDP's intangibles exceed its
equity, so tangible invested capital is negative.

**A basis warning that applies to every table.** On 18 December 2025 IDP made a
voluntary change to its Student Placement revenue recognition policy. FY25 is
restated throughout the FY26 accounts, and this file uses the **restated** FY25.
FY22 to FY24 are on the old basis and are not restated. Do not compute a growth rate
across the FY24/FY25 boundary.

# B. Industry block — a licence-dependent, government-rationed services business

IDP has no inventory, no commodity and no single customer. What it has instead is
four things the core block does not measure.

**1. The licence is the asset, and a government can withdraw it.**

IELTS earns because governments put it on an approved list. Australia went from five
accepted tests to nine on 7 August 2025, so IELTS volume can fall with no fall in
students. The UK is now buying a single supplier, and **the IELTS partnership
withdrew from that tender on 17 March 2026.** Track approval-list membership in all
four destinations as a balance sheet item, because that is what it is.

**2. Destination concentration.**

Australia is **58% of placements** in FY26, up from 43%, because the other corridors
collapsed rather than because Australia grew. A concentration that rises through
other people's losses is not diversification improving; it is the opposite. If
Australia's migration package lands, the concentration works against IDP twice —
volume falls, and the mix reverses, so yield falls with it.

**3. The effective tax rate, which is now a real cost.**

| FY | Pre-tax profit | Tax expense | Effective rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY24 | 188.80 | 55.27 | 29.3% |
| FY25 restated | 74.46 | 23.75 | 31.9% |
| FY26 | **40.62** | **27.33** | **67.3%** |

The FY26 rate is not a change of tax posture. It is **$15.7m of unrecoverable
withholding taxes and $6.1m of attributed foreign income**, which are largely
fixed-dollar items, divided by a collapsed pre-tax profit. **The consequence matters:
these costs do not fall when profit falls, so every further dollar of profit decline
costs more than 30 cents of net profit.** A $34.1m deferred tax asset is recognised
against losses; unrecognised losses are only $2.2m.

**4. Receivable quality in currency-controlled countries.**

Credit loss provisions ran **$15.1m in FY25 and $6.0m in FY26**, both treated as
adjusting items. IDP collects fees in countries that restrict moving money out. The
provision falling by more than half is a genuine improvement, and it is also the
second-largest adjusting item, so it flatters adjusted EBIT in both directions.

# C. How the company looks

IDP is a good business having its licence and its market withdrawn at the same time,
and it is being managed well through it.

The balance sheet is comfortable and is not the question. Net debt of $118.6m at one
times earnings, $341.1m undrawn, no maturity before April 2028, cash conversion of
139% and a register that has not moved in three years. IDP could fund a bad decade.
Time is not against it, which is precisely why waiting costs so little here.

Management is doing the one thing it can. $32m came out of costs in FY26 against a
$25m target, headcount fell by about 1,250 roles, test venues went from about 1,500
to under 600, and break-even revenue fell about $93m. That is not cost-cutting
theatre; it is a real reduction in the revenue at which the business survives, and
another $15m is planned.

The problem is the statutory account, and it is not a presentational quibble.
Statutory EBIT of $61.8m against invested capital of $517.5m is a return of 8.4%
against a cost of capital near 10%. On adjusted EBIT the return is 16.6% and the
business is excellent. **The entire investment case is the gap between those two
numbers**, and the gap is $61.1m of costs that have now recurred for two years with
$40m more scheduled. If the transformation ends, IDP is a fine business at 4.8 times
earnings before interest and tax. If "one-off" is what this company calls its normal
cost of operating in a shrinking market, then 8.4% is the truth and the shares are
not cheap.

Two smaller things sharpen it. The effective tax rate of 67% means profit falls
faster than pre-tax profit, so the downside compounds. And the FY27 guidance carries
an unanswered arithmetic problem: it assumes market volumes fall 20% to 30%, yet the
profit range implies revenue only about 7% below FY26. Either IDP takes a great deal
of share, or the guidance is soft.

The honest summary: **IDP is a well-run company with a strong balance sheet, priced
for a permanent decline, whose accounts will not say whether it is cheap until the
restructuring line stops.** That is a real question with a real date on it, which is
better than most of what a market offers.

# D. Threshold lines

These are what briefings quote. Each names the level at which the answer changes.

**1. ROIC against WACC — and which EBIT you believe.**
The cost of capital is about **10%**. Statutory ROIC is **8.4%** and vetoes BUY.
Adjusted ROIC is **16.6%** and supports it. The gap is $61.1m of adjusting items,
$36.2m of it restructuring now in its second year, with about $40m guided for FY27.

> **The line: the transformation cost line must stop.** FY27 is guided to carry about
> $40m, so FY28 is the first year that can be clean. **Until statutory ROIC clears
> 10%, the same rule applied to GEM applies here.** A partial test lands sooner: if
> FY27 restructuring comes in materially above $40m, the answer is already no.

**2. Covenant headroom — read it at the half, never at the year.**
The limit is **3.0x** net leverage on the banking group, adjusted for transformation
costs. At FY26 the borrower group ran 1.0x. But **at H1 FY26 it ran 2.0x** — two
thirds of the way to the limit — because the half carries the transformation spend.
The full-year figure flatters.

> **The line: half-year banking group leverage above 2.5x.** That is 83% of the limit
> with a half still to run. The next reading is the February 2027 result.
> **Re-verify the 3.0x itself at that result**, because the only source for it is a
> presentation from August 2025.

**3. Free cash flow against what it must fund.**
FY26 free cash flow was $103.1m. FY27 must cover about $40m of one-off costs, about
$21m of net interest, tax at a punitive effective rate, about $32m of capex, a 9.0c
dividend costing about $25m, and up to $50m of buyback — against adjusted EBIT
guided at $95m to $115m.

> **The line: FY27 free cash flow below about $30m.** Below it, the buyback and the
> dividend cannot both continue.

**4. The reverse read — what the price already assumes.**
Enterprise value is **$594.6m**. At 8 times EV/EBIT, which is unremarkable for a
service business with no growth, that price implies sustainable adjusted EBIT of
about **$74m** — roughly **22% below the bottom of management's own FY27 guidance**
of $95m, and permanently.

> **The market assumes adjusted EBIT settles at $60m to $75m and stays there.** BUY
> needs the chains to show volumes stabilising above that level. The first honest
> read is the February 2027 half; the AGM in late October is the first hint.

**5. Dilution — running the right way, for now.**
The $50m buyback retires about 10.5% of the register at $1.71. If it completes,
earnings per share rise about 12% on unchanged earnings.

> **The line: the pace, not just the stopping.** At the observed rate the program
> would spend well under $10m by its 30 June 2027 end, against "up to $50m". Read the
> pace as the board's own live opinion of the price: **buying that accelerates below
> $1.70 is price discipline and is bullish; buying that stays near zero while the
> price falls says the board wants the cash more than the shares.** Either way this is
> now a **weekly series, and it is the only company signal before the AGM.**

# E. Two standing cautions

**1. Rebuild yearly. Refresh net debt, leverage and cover at each half-year.** Those
three move, and they are the ones that kill you. For IDP the half-year refresh
matters more than for most, because the transformation spend makes the half
materially worse than the year, and the covenant is tested against the worse number.
The next refresh is the H1 FY27 result in late February 2027; the next full rebuild
is after the FY27 result in August 2027.

**2. The definition of invested capital is fixed at the top of this file and must not
be switched.** NOPAT is statutory EBIT after tax at 30%. Invested capital is equity
plus net debt excluding leases, with goodwill included. Statutory, not adjusted, is
the base — the adjusted figure is reported beside it so the gap stays visible, never
in place of it. A ROIC that moves because the method moved is worse than no ROIC.
