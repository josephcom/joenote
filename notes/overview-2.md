# Overview
## How does IDP earn?
IDP sells three things and bills three different people.

1. **Student placement.** IDP counsels a student, applies to universities on their behalf, and the **university** pays the commission — a share of the first year's tuition, or a flat fee per enrolment. The student usually pays IDP nothing.
2. **IELTS.** The **test taker** pays. IDP is one of three co-owners of IELTS, with the British Council and Cambridge, and delivers the test in about 60 countries, including all of India.
3. **English teaching.** IDP runs language schools in Cambodia and Taiwan. **Students** pay tuition. It is small — 5% of revenue.

A fourth line sits inside placement. IDP Connect sells marketing, events and data to universities on contract, not per student.

ASX:IEL = IDP Education. Revenue is three multiplications and one contract book:

> **revenue** = placements × commission per placement + tests × fee per test + course enrolments × course fee, plus IDP Connect contracts

FY26 was 54,200 placements at $5,586 each, 1,195,400 tests at $325 each, and 108,400 course enrolments at about $366 each.

**Look at what is on each side.** Every volume term is pure *demand*. Every yield term is pure *price*, and IDP sets it. There is no capacity term at all, because capacity has never been the limit: in FY26 IDP cut its IELTS venues from about 1,500 to fewer than 600 and its headcount by 1,250 people (20%), and still served every customer it wanted.

The *supply* constraint in this business is not IDP's. It belongs to six foreign governments. Australia sets a National Planning Level of **295,000** new student commencements. Canada plans for **155,000** study permit arrivals. Those are quotas. When a government rations student visas, the market's supply is fixed by law, and no amount of demand can lift IDP's volume above it.

So both sides are outside the company's control, and both are shrinking. Demand falls because a foreign degree costs more and returns less. Supply falls because governments cut it. Yield is the only number IDP owns, and it has raised it every single year through the collapse.

**NOTE** — IDP restated FY25 on 18 December 2025 after a voluntary revenue-recognition change. Restated FY25 placements are about 74,300, not 70,100. Management quotes FY26 changes against the restated base, so its "−27%" and "Australia −9%" do not reconcile with the reported row above. Pick one base and stay with it.

The cause is policy, in all four large destinations at once.

* **Australia** grants fewer visas and refuses more. Offshore grants ran about **193,000** in FY2025-26, −8%, the lowest since 2016-17. In **July 2026** higher-education grants were **−40%** on July 2025, at a 66% offshore grant rate — the worst July in at least 21 years. Evidence levels for India, Nepal, Bangladesh and Bhutan were raised in January 2026, and offshore grant rates that month ran India 48%, Bangladesh 19%, Nepal 13%.
* **Canada** capped itself. About **73,800** new study permits were issued in 2025 against a target of 305,900 — 24% of it. IDP's Canada placements fell **80%** in FY26.
* **The United States** issued **34%** fewer F-1 visas in 2025 than 2024, with India down 76% against 2023. IDP's US placements fell **67%**.
* **The United Kingdom** granted **383,455** sponsored study visas in the year to June 2026, −11%, and **−41%** against the 2023 peak.

India is the hinge. It is **37%** of IDP's placements, up from 31%, and its largest testing market. Indian students going abroad fell from about 908,000 in 2023 to **626,000 in 2025**. India IELTS volumes fell 50% in FY25 and a further 22% in FY26.

## Operating Leverage
Revenue less a stable 58-64% gross margin, less adjusted overheads, less about $52m of other fixed cost. IDP does not publish a fixed/variable split, so the last three columns are my derivation.

| Year | Revenue | Gross margin | Fixed cost | Adj. EBIT | True leverage | Break-even revenue |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| FY22 | $793.3m | 57.9% | $296.3m | $163.2m | 2.8× | $512m |
| FY23 | $981.9m | 62.5% | $386.1m | $227.8m | 2.7× | $618m |
| FY24 | $1,037.2m | 63.9% | $423.4m | $239.4m | 2.8× | $663m |
| FY25 | $882.2m | 59.6% | $406.8m | $119.0m | 4.4× | $683m |
| FY26 | $795.4m | 60.0% | $354.3m | $122.9m | 3.9× | **$590m** |

FY25 is the year the leverage bit: revenue fell 15% and adjusted EBIT fell 50%. FY26 broke that pattern. Revenue fell 11% and adjusted EBIT *rose* 3%, because $50m came out of overheads and yield rose 11%. That is the single most important fact in the FY26 result, and it is why the break-even line moved down $93m in one year. Another $15m of cost-out is planned for FY27, which takes break-even to about $565m — roughly 29% below FY26 revenue.

Against that, statutory profit is thin. FY26 statutory NPAT was **$13.3m** against adjusted NPAT of $57.1m. The $61.1m of "significant items" is mostly restructuring, and IDP has guided to about **$40m more of it in FY27**. The restructuring is not one-off yet.

FY27 guidance is adjusted EBIT of **$95–115m**, on an assumption that market volumes run **20-30% below FY26**. On the same margin and the planned cost-out, the midpoint implies revenue near $740m — only 7% below FY26. Either IDP expects to take share, or yield and mix are meant to carry it. That is my arithmetic, not a company disclosure.

* Marked **e** = estimate. Only IDP publishes hard test numbers. Pearson publishes a percentage change and nothing else (−10% in 2024, −5% in 2025, −3% in the first half of 2026). Duolingo publishes test revenue ($42.0m in 2025) and no volume. ETS publishes score tables and no volume at all. Any IELTS market share is a derivation.
* Cambridge owns the test design and does not deliver tests. The three owners hold IELTS jointly. **IDP has never published its stake, the revenue split or any royalty rate.** The "one third each" figure comes from trade press, not from the company.
* IDP bought the British Council's India IELTS business for **£130m in 2021** and is now the only IELTS provider in India. India volumes have since fallen by more than half. The asset carries no impairment.
* IDP entered **China** in December 2024, ending the British Council's monopoly there. It had 13 test centres at June 2026 and targets 25 in early FY27. It is the one place IDP is adding capacity.

**Placement share.** Australia recorded 214,100 higher-education commencements in 2025. IDP placed 31,700 students into Australia in FY26, so it touches roughly **15%** of that flow. My derivation. IDP places into six destinations and does not publish a share for any of them.

**The peers.**
* **Crizac** (India-listed, IPO July 2025) is the only listed pure comparable. The UK is 97% of its revenue. In the year to March 2026 its revenue rose **22.7%**, applications rose 43%, and its share of UK study visas went from 3.5% to **6.0%** — while IDP shrank 11%. Then in the June 2026 quarter Crizac's income fell 4%, its first fall. Read the sequence: part of IDP's FY26 decline was share loss, and by mid-2026 the whole market was going down together.
* **Pearson** is losing PTE volume more slowly than IDP is losing IELTS volume. That gap is share.
* **Duolingo's** test is cheap and growing nowhere. It is on **none** of the Australian, Canadian or UK government approved lists. Governments rejecting at-home tests is IELTS's main defence.
* **The British Council** is a co-owner, not a rival, and it is in trouble: a £51m loss in 2024-25, a **£197m** government loan with no principal repaid since April 2024, and no return to profit expected before 2029-30. It delivers close to 90% of the world's UK-visa English tests, and the UK Home Office is running a **£1.13bn** procurement to move that to a single supplier.
* The placement channel is consolidating. **Study Group** went into administration in May 2026 and was sold to Arete Education. **INTO** is loss-making at several of its university joint ventures. **ApplyBoard** cut more than 150 staff in June 2025.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt **$118.6m**, net leverage **1.0×**, $341m undrawn, facilities maturing 2028, 2029 and 2030. Cash conversion 139%. A $50m buyback runs to 30 June 2027. Debt is not the near-term risk. IDP left the S&P/ASX 200 on 22 June 2026.
