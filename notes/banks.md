# Banks
Banks usually use a collateral like your shares portfolio and lend k% of its value V. What you owe to bank is now your debt or D. As you pay your installment D becomes smaller and smaller. If the value of your portfolio (V) goes down, then you have to force sell the shares untill D/V becomes k% again. The formula is:
> V_new = V − (D − kV)/(1 − k)
The above formula works only when D > kV

**Example**
You have 100 shares at $1 => V = $100. Bank lends k = 80% => D=80%*$100=**$80**. Share price goes down to $0.90 => V = $90. Now D > kV ($80 > 80%*$90), so you must sell.

## Fraud 

when banks want to lend money, they analyse their customers' financials to see how risky they are. Accordingly, they adjust whether or not they will lend, and if yes, at what interest. This is why some companies, especially capital hungry ones, sometimes are tempted to artificially keep their share prices high and pump up their revenue/profit. As one example, Adani Group allegedly inject its money to secondary companies (offshore, belonging to their family members), then ask them to buy shares and products so share prices and revenues look better! A circle!

When they are caught red handed, large investors (and people too) naturally run for the exit because fund managers just want to save their position and face; they don't really care too much about whether or not the accusation is real.
