# Leading Indicators

## Market Level

- Headline & trimmed-mean CPI (monthly ABS) — 3.8% / 3.6% to June 2026. Root of the whole rate chain.
- (CPI ->) RBA cash rate — 4.35%, +75bp across 2026 after −75bp in 2025. Next decision 28–29 Sep.
- (CPI -> cash rate ->) variable mortgage rate and average repayment — under 5% of Australian mortgages are fixed, so pass-through is near-total within weeks. Fastest transmission in the developed world.
- (CPI -> cash rate -> mortgage rate ->) mortgage stress — 28.5% of borrowers "at risk", 19.8% "extremely at risk", concentrated in exactly the low-to-middle-income young-family cohort that buys childcare.
- (CPI -> cash rate -> mortgage stress ->) household saving ratio and monthly household spending — 6.2% and falling; families are funding consumption from buffers, which run out.
- Wage Price Index vs CPI = real wage growth — 3.2% vs 3.8%, i.e. **negative**. This is the number that decides whether a second income is worth the daily gap fee.
- (cash rate ->) ABS job vacancies — 329,500, first fall since Aug 2025. Leads employment ~2 quarters.
- (cash rate -> vacancies ->) unemployment and underemployment — 4.5% / 6.4%. Lags rates 3–6 quarters.
- (cash rate -> unemployment ->) participation rate — 66.9%, down 0.2pp.
- Consumer sentiment (Westpac–Melbourne Institute) — 88.9. "Time to buy a major household item" sits 35 points below its long-run average.
- (cash rate ->) house prices — national −0.7% in July 2026, biggest monthly fall since Dec 2022; Brisbane/Perth booms rolling over.
- Advertised rents and rental vacancy rate — +5.9% YoY, 1.6% vacancy. Competes for the same household dollar as the gap fee.
- Net overseas migration and permanent migration caps — NOM 306k in 2024–25, down from 429k; program 185,000 places; student cap lifted to 295,000.
- Return-to-office mandates — average 3.64 mandated office days in 2026, up from 3.43 in 2024; 39% of employers require five days.
- (federal budget ->) fiscal position and stance — $31.5bn deficit 2026–27, judged "neutral to mildly expansionary". Sets the headroom for the subsidies that fund ~70% of sector revenue.
- Electoral cycle — Labor holds 94 seats, next election due by July 2028. No policy-reversal risk inside a normal horizon.
- Business credit spreads and small-cap risk appetite — spreads near 2022 lows, but Small Ordinaries −11% YTD while the ASX 200 prints records. Affects refinancing and any equity raise, not revenue directly.

### Notes

Macro reaches GEM almost entirely *through* the sector layer, so treat these as second-derivative. Two things worth knowing: childcare is **stickier than most discretionary spend on the way down** (a place is hard to re-secure, so families hold it through short unemployment spells), and the macro squeeze shows up first in **days booked per child**, not in children enrolled. Also note the macro pushes both ways — rising mortgages and rents *increase* the financial need for a second income, which supports demand, while simultaneously making the gap fee harder to pay.

## Sector Level

**Demand feedstock**

- (cash rate -> housing and living costs ->) births and total fertility rate — 292,318 births in 2024, TFR 1.481 (record low), projected 1.42 in 2025–26. Leads 0–2 rooms ~1–2 years, 3–5 rooms ~3–4 years.
- (births ->) 0–5 population cohort — ~1.8m children against ~840,000 approved places (0.47 places per child).
- (births ->) second and third-child births specifically — falling fertility hits revenue twice, because a second child brings both an extra enrolment *and* the higher 95% second-child CCS rate.
- (migration caps ->) family-forming arrivals (permanent + NZ visas, ~141k) as distinct from student visas — the childcare-relevant migration stream is shrinking faster than headline NOM suggests.
- (unemployment -> participation ->) female and parental workforce participation — 63.2%, off its record high; women 30–34 already at ~81%, so the historic structural tailwind is largely spent.
- (return-to-office mandates ->) average weekly hours booked per child — 27.8 hrs and rising. Currently the only demand metric moving the right way.
- Paid Parental Leave extension to 26 weeks (1 July 2026) — pushes back entry into the 0–1 room, the highest-ratio and hardest-to-fill cohort.
- Quarterly CCS data, children in approved care YoY — **−2.1% at March Q 2026**. The cleanest demand read available; ~3 month publication lag.

**Price and government funding**

- (federal budget ->) CCS hourly rate cap and its July indexation — $15.19 from 6 July 2026, +3.8%.
- (federal budget ->) CCS income thresholds — 90% rate to $88,520, zero above $538,520.
- 3 Day Guarantee (activity test abolished 5 Jan 2026) — 72 subsidised hours/fortnight regardless of activity. Real but small: ~66,700 families, ~6.7% of the CCS base, and it has produced no visible enrolment inflection.
- (Worker Retention Payment ->) fee growth cap — 4.4% → 4.2% → **5.8% for 8 Aug 2026–7 Aug 2027**. This is a grant condition, not a legal ceiling. G8 can breach it, or re-price mid-year, if it gives up the WRP cash. After the final program year the cap disappears and fee growth is unconstrained.
- (CCS cap indexation ~3.8% vs gross fee growth ~4.3% ->) **out-of-pocket childcare CPI — +7.6% to June 2026, roughly 2× headline**. The affordability signal that actually predicts enrolment loss.
- Share of services charging above the CCS cap — ~39%. Above the cap every extra dollar is 100% out-of-pocket with zero subsidy offset.
- (award review + WRP ->) Children's Services Award wage steps — new 8-level structure from 1 Mar 2026, staged rises 30 Jun 2026/2027/2028/2029, plus Annual Wage Review +4.75% from 1 Jul 2026.
- Early Education Service Delivery Prices project (Deloitte; data collection closed Aug 2026) — precursor to a benchmark or regulated price. The single biggest open regime risk.
- National ECEC Commission (consultation from May 2026) — would introduce supply planning for the first time. Potentially a *positive* for incumbents if it throttles new entrants.
- Building Early Education Fund ($1bn, incl. school co-location) — taxpayer-funded new competitor supply.

**Supply and competition**

- **CBDC service count YoY (+2.9%) minus children in care YoY (−2.1%) — a ~5pp/year occupancy drag.** The best single sector occupancy predictor there is.
- ACECQA quarterly service count — 18,197 at 1 Jul 2026, the first net quarterly fall (−10) in the series. Watch for a second.
- (cash rate -> construction costs + property yields -> development feasibility ->) childcare development applications — DAs falling ~5%/yr, and only ~40 of every 100 become operational within four years. A 2–4 year lead on competitor supply.
- (cash rate ->) childcare property cap rates and transaction volumes — cheap capital funds new centres; rising yields choke the pipeline.
- National LDC occupancy — 76.1% at Dec 2025 vs 81.8% at Dec 2023. ~70% is the viability floor.
- State occupancy dispersion — WA 70.5% (−11.8pp in two years after ~17.6% place growth), NSW 80.3%, QLD 79.4%.
- Small-provider insolvencies and closures — the sector's self-correction; more closures means better occupancy for survivors.
- (educator wage rises ->) staffing waiver rate and educator vacancies — waivers 8.9% → 4.3%, vacancies −31%. Labour is no longer the binding constraint; demand is.

**Regulation and trust**

- Sector-wide child safety media cycles — the 2025 Victorian case reset parent trust nationally, not just at the operator involved.
- (child safety reform ->) compliance and enforcement action counts — 574 (Q1 2025) → 933 (Q1 2026), **+63%**.
- (child safety reform ->) Commonwealth power to cancel CCS approval (live from 1 Jul 2026) — 115 services on notice, 7 surrendered, 1 defunded.
- New national law from 27 Feb 2026 — worker register, personal device ban, mandatory training (deadline 27 Aug 2026), penalties tripled, regulators able to suspend a provider for "systemic risk".
- (WRP extension ->) QA2 health-and-safety rating condition from July 2027 — a sub-standard rating now directly cuts wage-subsidy cash flow.

**State preschool programs**

- Victoria Free Kinder and Pre-Prep hours (16–25 hrs in 2026, scaling to 30 by 2036); QLD Free Kindy; SA 3-year-old preschool from 2026; NSW Start Strong rates ($2,563 for LDC vs $4,456 for community preschools).

### Notes

The dominant sector fact is a **scissor**: supply growing ~3%/yr into demand shrinking ~2%/yr. Nothing in policy currently closes that gap — the 3 Day Guarantee is too small, and the Building Early Education Fund actively widens it.

The second key fact is the **out-of-pocket CPI at +7.6%**. Government affordability headlines and provider fee-pressure complaints are both true: the subsidy cap indexes at 3.8% while gross fees rise 4.3%, so the uncovered gap compounds — and for the ~39% of services already above the cap there's no offset at all. That's the most plausible driver of the −2.1% enrolment decline after births.

On state preschool: these programs are delivered *through* long day care, so they're mostly fee-substitution revenue and an enrolment magnet, not a competitor. The real risk is **age mix** — expanding funded hours in the 3–5 band (profitable, 1:11 ratio) while births fall in the 0–3 band (loss-making, 1:4 ratio) is the worst possible combination for room economics. Watch that mechanism, not the "free kindy" headlines.

## Company Level

- **Group spot occupancy at each disclosure (Feb / late-Apr AGM / Aug / Nov)** — the master metric. Management's own translation is **~$4–5m of EBIT per 1 percentage point**. FY25 average 65.8%; Feb-26 spot 54.4%; Apr-26 spot 56.4%.
- **The February spot reading specifically** — the January intake result, published ~10 months ahead of the revenue it drives. October 2025's failed seasonal lift was the exact trigger for the Nov-25 guidance downgrade.
- (sector supply + births + trust damage ->) **the gap between GEM occupancy and national LDC occupancy** — currently ~10–20pp. This is the cleanest way to separate GEM-specific damage from sector damage.
- (abuse charges -> media cycle -> parent trust ->) **enquiry volume, and separately enquiry-to-enrolment conversion** — management's stated demand mechanism. In H1-25 enquiries held and conversion fell; by Feb-26 enquiries themselves fell. That shift from a *price* problem to a *trust* problem is the key qualitative signal.
- (abuse charges ->) Joshua Brown County Court calendar — 153 charges, committed 28 May 2026, indicated intention to plead. Each hearing has reliably produced a news cycle and an enrolment response.
- (occupancy ->) live centre count on StartingBlocks.gov.au — 395 at Dec-25 → ~351 now. Updates continuously, ahead of any ASX announcement. Free and real-time.
- (occupancy ->) further suspensions beyond the 40 announced 29 Apr 2026 (VIC 12, WA 12, NSW 7, QLD 5, SA 4).
- GEM's January fee decision — by practice one rise a year with 14 days' notice, self-imposed at 4.2% (now 5.8%) to keep WRP money. Nothing stops a second rise, or a bigger one. Price is usually pre-known, but treat it as a choice G8 makes, not a fixed input.
- State and brand occupancy mix — VIC and WA carry the damage, NSW/QLD much less. Network at Dec-25: NSW 137, VIC 128, QLD 60, WA 46, SA 24.
- GEM-specific enforcement actions and licence conditions on StartingBlocks — 64 enforcement actions, 102 conditions; WA disproportionate (19 of 33 services flagged).
- VECRA criminal charges against G8 itself — 5 charges over Community Kids Hoppers Crossing, filed Jan 2026, max ~$197k — plus any further state prosecutions.
- Fair Work Ombudsman Federal Court action — filed 8 Jul 2026, 1,400+ workers, >$2m alleged underpayments. No first hearing listed.
- Individual family civil claims (100+ families, deliberately not a class action) and any contingent liability, provision or insurance disclosure — currently unquantified. **No shareholder class action has been filed over the 2025–26 disclosure.**
- NQS ratings profile — 95% meeting or exceeding, 4pp above sector. QA2 specifically now gates WRP funding from Jul 2027.
- Staff retention, vacancy rate, time-to-hire — 79% retention, vacancies −33%, time-to-hire −14%.
- Net Promoter Score (53, a 3-year high) and employee engagement (76%).
- Seek job-ad counts by state — the cheapest real-time read on whether GEM is staffing for recovery or further shrinkage.
- Balance sheet headroom — net debt $117m, leverage 1.18x, covenants compliant, $100m revolver extended to Jan 2029. Determines how long loss-making centres can be carried.
- Landlord posture and rent as % of revenue — 396 of 398 centres are leased, escalations fixed at 3–4%, and rent is still payable on the suspended sites. Management, bluntly: "no landlord is prepared to do anything to help."
- Capex (~$50m FY26) and greenfield openings — **zero new centres opened in FY25**. No new revenue capacity coming.
- Substantial holder notices, especially Allan Gray at 19.87% — one notice away from a control question.
- Board and management changes — COO changed July 2026; MD Okhovat backed at the April 2026 AGM; FY25 STI nil, 2023 LTIP vesting deferred.
- **H1 2026 result, 25 August 2026** — the next hard data point, two days away.

### Notes

Two counter-intuitive points here.

First, the **usual operating leading indicators are inverted at GEM**. Retention, vacancies, NQS ratings, NPS and engagement all *improved* while occupancy fell 8pp. Agency labour is effectively zero (<0.08% of hours), so it's useless as a signal. These metrics will not warn you about revenue — but they do tell you the demand loss isn't a service-quality problem, which matters for whether it's recoverable.

Second, **the reported half-year is stale on arrival**. GEM runs a calendar year, so the 25 August release covers January–June. The *spot* occupancy line in it is worth far more than the reported half.

Seasonality is severe and worth internalising: late January the 4–5 cohort leaves for school, February is the annual trough, October is normally the peak. February effectively sets the year.

## Other Notes

**What GEM does not disclose** — average daily fee, the government-vs-parent revenue split, licensed places affected by the 40 suspensions, and any contingent liability for the abuse claims. The ~70% CCS share of revenue is a sector average, not a G8 disclosure. Treat any per-place fee figure as a derivation.

**Watch out for definition traps.** 65.8% is an *annual average*; 54–56% is a *February spot* at the seasonal trough — they are not comparable. Sector "centre counts" also differ wildly by source (~9,750 LDC centres vs 9,920 CCS-approved CBDC services vs 17,820 ACECQA centre-based services).

**What is NOT a revenue indicator, despite looking like one:** the share price, the Small Ords index, credit spreads, and the goodwill impairment. Those affect the multiple and the financing, not the top line. The impairment was non-cash and explicitly did not breach covenants.

**Best free real-time trackers** — StartingBlocks.gov.au for GEM's live service count and enforcement/condition tallies; ACECQA quarterly NQF Snapshot for sector supply; the Department of Education quarterly CCS data report for the demand-vs-supply scissor; ABS monthly CPI for the childcare component.

**Calendar to diarise:** GEM H1 result 25 Aug 2026 · ABS July CPI 26 Aug · ABS Births (2025 data) ~Oct 2026 · June-Q CCS data report ~Oct 2026 · RBA 28–29 Sep, 2–3 Nov, 7–8 Dec · MYEFO Dec 2026 · GEM FY26 result and the critical February spot occupancy, late Feb 2027 · WRP QA2 condition bites Jul 2027 · WRP funding cliff 30 Jun 2028.

**One live gap:** the outcome of the 23 June 2026 County Court directions hearing in the Brown matter isn't publicly reported, and no plea or sentencing date is set. That's an unpriced news-cycle catalyst.
