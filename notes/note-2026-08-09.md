# Leverage and Profit Margin

## Margin is what's left in your pocket

You sell a pie for $10. It costs you $7. You keep $3.

Margin = $3 out of $10 = **30%**. That's it.

## Costs come in two flavours

**Fixed** — you pay it whether you sell one pie or a thousand. Rent. Wages. Insurance.

**Variable** — you only pay it when you actually sell something. The meat in the pie.

This split is the whole game. Most people never look at it.

## Operating leverage: the amplifier

Two pie shops. Both make **$100** a year. Both keep **$10** profit. Same margin. Look identical on paper.

**VOLATILE_PIES** — rent and wages $80, meat $10.
**STABLE_PIES** — rent and wages $10, meat $80.

Now sales drop 10%. Both take **$90**.

**VOLATILE_PIES:** meat drops to $9 (sold less). Rent still $80.
$90 − $80 − $9 = **$1 profit.**
Sales fell 10%. Profit fell **90%**.

**STABLE_PIES:** meat drops to $72. Fixed still $10.
$90 − $10 − $72 = **$8 profit.**
Sales fell 10%. Profit fell **20%**.

Same margin. Same drop. One shop got wiped out, the other barely felt it.

That's **operating leverage**, also called operating gearing.

## It works both ways

Sales go **up** 10% instead:

VOLATILE_PIES: **$19 profit.** Up 90%.
STABLE_PIES: **$12 profit.** Up 20%.

High leverage isn't bad. It's just loud. Good news and bad news both get shouted.

## How far can you fall?

Where does profit hit zero?

> **Break-even sales = Fixed costs ÷ ((Sales − Variable costs) ÷ Sales)**

Three numbers, all straight off the books.

That bracket has a real name — **contribution margin ratio** — but you don't need the name to use it.

**VOLATILE_PIES.** Sales $100, meat $10. ($100 − $10) ÷ $100 = 0.90. Rent $80.
$80 ÷ 0.90 = **$89 of sales.**
You die at $89 — you can only lose **11%**.

**STABLE_PIES.** Sales $100, meat $80. ($100 − $80) ÷ $100 = 0.20. Rent $10.
$10 ÷ 0.20 = **$50 of sales.**
You die at $50 — you can lose **50%**.

That gap is your **margin of safety**. It decides who's still trading in three years.

## Who has what, and why it's normal

**High leverage — mostly fixed costs**
Airlines, cinemas, gyms, hotels, childcare, toll roads, telcos, miners.

Why: the plane flies whether it's full or empty. You can't half-build a runway or hire half a lifeguard. Huge cost to set up, cheap to run one more customer. Normal for the industry — not a red flag on its own.

**Low leverage — mostly variable costs**
Supermarkets, wholesalers, retailers, builders, labour hire, consultants.

Why: their biggest cost is the stuff they buy in. No sales, no stock bought. No job, no worker hired. Costs shrink with them.

## The bit that matters

Toll roads have massive leverage and are dead safe — because traffic barely moves.

Danger isn't the amplifier. Danger is **big amplifier plus wobbly revenue**.

So ask two questions, never one:

1. How big is the amplifier?
2. How steady is the revenue?
