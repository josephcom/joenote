**The Capital Cycle**

Something good happens in a sector. Returns go up.

High returns attract more players. New money builds new capacity. The new capacity takes years to arrive, so it arrives late — and it arrives all at once, because every player saw the same good news.

More players now eat from the same pie. Returns go down.

Low returns scare away players. New building stops. Weak players leave, but slowly, because capacity does not disappear easily.

Fewer players eat from the same pie. Returns go up again.

The cycle repeats.

Two rules control the cycle:

1. Capital goes where returns are high.
2. Capital moves slowly.

Rule 2 is why the cycle overshoots. Supply does not stop at the correct level. It goes past it, in both directions.

The important point is this: watch supply, not demand. Demand gives you a surprise. Supply does not. Supply announces itself years before it arrives — in permits, in orders, in construction data. You can count it.

Two things weaken the cycle. A moat protects returns from new players. A structural change makes a decline permanent, not temporary.
